How UV ink actually cures

UV ink does not dry — it cures. A photoinitiator absorbs light around 395 nm and locks the film into a cross-linked network. Here is why touch-dry is not the same as cured, and what energy density really means.

How a piezo printhead forms a droplet

A piezo head does not boil ink or spray it — it deforms a chamber and sends a pressure wave through the nozzle. Drop size, grey-scale levels and dot gain all come from that waveform, which is why two machines with the same head can print differently.

Why dark and clear materials need white ink

CMYK inks are translucent. On black acrylic or clear PET the colour has nothing to reflect back, so it turns muddy or disappears. White ink builds the reflective base — and because it is a pigment suspension, it needs circulation, not just a good profile.

Varnish and 2.5D relief: building height on purpose

Varnish is a build material, not just a shine. Spot varnish adds contrast; multiple passes add real height. The hard part is curing the middle of the stack and keeping dust out of a wet, glossy surface.

UV foil stamping: glue, heat and a very short window

UV foil is not a metallic ink. A tacky glue is printed exactly where the foil should stick, the foil is pressed on with heat and pressure, and the carrier is peeled away. Most failures are a glue or a temperature problem, not a foil problem.

UV DTF crystal labels: print the sticker, not the part

UV DTF prints the image, white and adhesive onto a film, laminates it, and transfers the whole stack by hand. It is the practical answer for curved, small or already-finished parts that cannot go on a flatbed.

360° cylinder printing: it is a fixturing problem first

A rotary UV printer wraps the image around a rotating part while the head moves along the axis. Diameter, taper and runout decide the gap; the seam decides whether the job looks professional. Measure the part before you print it.

Adhesion and surface energy: the dyne number decides

UV ink sticks when it wets the surface. If the substrate's surface energy is below the ink's surface tension, the ink beads up and peels, no matter how much UV energy you give it. Clean, then treat, then test.

Pass lines and banding: find the cause before you touch the profile

Pass lines are a registration error, not a colour error. A mechanical machine always has some error; the job is to keep it below the visible threshold. Nozzle check, alignment, feathering and step — in that order.

A colour shift after months of good output is usually a physical change — ink lot, head condition, lamp output, temperature or media — not a broken profile. Measure density first; re-profile last.
