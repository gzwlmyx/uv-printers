Printhead care: the habits that decide head life

Most dead printheads are killed by maintenance habits, not by printing hours. Cap it, wipe it, flush it, keep the cleaning liquid out of it, and never point an air gun at it.

  • printhead maintenance
  • nozzle check
  • capping
  • flushing

The short answer: a UV printhead fails from dried ink, air, physical impact or contamination far more often than from wear. The daily routine takes ten minutes; the repair it prevents costs a head and a day of production. If you only do one thing, cap the head properly every time the machine stops for more than a few minutes.

DoDon't
Print a nozzle check before the first job and after any stop longer than an hourDon't start a paying job on an unverified head
Wipe the nozzle plate with the correct wiper and a little maintenance liquid, in one directionDon't scrub with dry paper or a dirty cloth
Keep the cap station clean and the cap seal softDon't let ink pool and dry in the cap
Flush before changing ink type or brandDon't leave cleaning liquid in the head for days
Ground the machine and mind static in dry weatherDon't point a high-pressure air gun at the nozzle
Power off before unplugging or moving a headDon't force a head into position by hand

The eight habits that kill heads

  1. Removing or reseating a head with the power on.
  2. Leaving cleaning liquid inside the head for more than 48 hours.
  3. Changing ink without flushing — even the same colour from another brand.
  4. Running cheap ink or cheap cleaning fluid.
  5. Blasting the nozzle plate with a high-pressure air gun.
  6. Cleaning without protecting the circuit board and connectors.
  7. Adjusting head position by force instead of the proper screws and datum.
  8. Ignoring ESD protection when handling the head or the boards.

When a nozzle is missing

Start with a nozzle check and a clean. If the line comes back, log it and move on; if it returns within the same shift, the cause is upstream — air in the damper, a partly blocked filter, a weak cap seal — and cleaning will not fix it. Soaking the nozzle plate is a last resort, not a routine. A head that has lost a nozzle permanently is a replacement decision; keep printing on a known-defective head and you will scrap good media.

Field note

Workshop note: humidity is part of head care. Below about 35 % RH, ink at the nozzle dries faster and static pulls dust onto the plate; above 65 %, condensation and media curl become the problem. Our machines are specified for 35–65 % RH for good reason, and a cheap hygrometer on the wall has saved us more heads than any cleaning kit.

One more economic point: consumables with a known service life — ink tubes, dampers, cap seals, limit switches — are cheaper to replace on schedule than to replace after they fail mid-job. Preventive replacement is the least glamorous and most profitable maintenance habit we have.
