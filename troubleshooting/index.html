Fault finder

What is the machine telling you?

Start from the symptom, not from the part. Each entry lists the likely causes, what to do first, and how to stop it coming back. Everything below is readable without JavaScript.

Machine type

12 fault patterns

FIG. 01Dark or light bands appear at regular intervals in solid fills and gradients (pass lines / banding).HighUV flatbed, roll-to-roll

Likely causes

  • Nozzle deflection, partial dropout or ink mist from one head
  • Bidirectional alignment off
  • Step (media feed) too small or too large
  • Media not flat or head height measured from the wrong point
  • Feathering set too low for the media and artwork (a symptom hider, not a cure)

First fixes

  1. Print a nozzle check at an angle under a lamp; clean or map the head
  2. Re-run bidirectional alignment at the real head height
  3. Calibrate the feed and check the Y belt / pinch rollers
  4. Re-measure head height and flatten the media
  5. Only then adjust feathering, and verify on a solid patch

Prevention

Print a nozzle check before the first job of the day, and log head height, alignment result and media batch with every job.

FIG. 02Output that matched last month now looks green, dull or lighter, especially in greys and skin tones.HighUV flatbed, roll-to-roll, rotary

Likely causes

  • Ink lot change, expired ink, or ink mixed from different brands without flushing
  • Partial nozzle dropout lowering density in one channel
  • UV lamp output decay (under-cured ink looks dull)
  • Media lot change, head height change, or room temperature outside 25–28 °C
  • Corrupted or overwritten ICC profile

First fixes

  1. Compare a known-good reference print and a density patch; identify which channel moved
  2. Nozzle check and clean; check lamp hours against the service record with a radiometer
  3. Reseat the known-good profile and reprint the reference before re-profiling
  4. Only rebuild the ICC after the machine is proven stable

Prevention

Record ink lot, media batch, lamp hours and head height on every job, and keep a physical reference print from the day the profile was verified.

FIG. 03Thin streaks in the print direction, or a fine mist of ink settling on the substrate and around the head, while the nozzle check looks acceptable.MediumUV flatbed, roll-to-roll

Likely causes

  • Slight nozzle clogging that only shows at high firing frequency
  • Ink viscosity off (temperature too low or too high), causing the tail to break badly
  • Head height too high, so droplets are carried by air currents
  • Static on the media pulling the mist onto the surface

First fixes

  1. Clean the head and reprint a 5 % and a 20 % patch, not only the nozzle check
  2. Bring the room and ink to the working range (25–28 °C on our machines)
  3. Lower the head to the correct gap and re-check alignment
  4. Wipe the media with IPA, use an ionising bar or raise humidity to 35–65 % RH

Prevention

Keep the machine within its humidity range, do a daily nozzle check, and clean the head area at the end of each shift.

FIG. 04Ink peels, flakes or lifts at the edges; tape test fails; the print survives the bench but not handling.HighUV flatbed, rotary, DTF

Likely causes

  • Surface energy below the ink's surface tension (PP/PE, contaminated metal)
  • Mould release agent, finger oil, dust or condensation left on the part
  • Primer too thick, or printed before it fully dried
  • Under-cured ink, especially thick white or varnish layers
  • Ink not suited to the substrate (rigid ink on a flexible part)

First fixes

  1. Test with a dyne pen; clean with IPA and a clean lint-free cloth, then let it flash off
  2. Apply a thin, even primer and dry it fully; or use flame / corona / plasma for low-energy plastics
  3. Verify cure with a thumbnail twist and tape test after the part cools
  4. Switch to a flexible ink and test a bend
  5. Do not promise durability before a 24-hour adhesion test on the real part

Prevention

Ask for the actual production material, print a proof, and record the treatment (clean / primer / flame) used for that substrate.

FIG. 05White prints weak, streaky or blue-grey; colour on top of it looks muddy; white nozzle checks keep dropping out after idle time.HighUV flatbed, rotary, DTF

Likely causes

  • White pigment settled in the cartridge, damper or line during idle time
  • Circulation or agitation not running
  • White ink past its shelf life or stored too cold
  • Ink limit too low for the required opacity, or a single heavy pass that cannot cure through

First fixes

  1. Run white circulation, purge the white channel, and re-check with a white patch on black
  2. Print two controlled white passes instead of one heavy pass
  3. Replace old ink; keep stock sealed and within the specified temperature range
  4. Clean or replace the white damper/filter if purging does not recover flow

Prevention

Keep white circulation on through the working day; run a white purge before the first job after any long stop; record ink lot on white-heavy jobs.

FIG. 06Varnish looks dull instead of glossy, or stays slightly tacky and marks when stacked.MediumUV flatbed

Likely causes

  • Under-cured varnish, often a thick build cured only at the surface
  • Varnish applied over ink that was not fully cured (interlayer problem)
  • Dust or orange-peel on the surface
  • Wrong varnish type for the required abrasion resistance
  • Lamp output decay or head height too high

First fixes

  1. Thin the build, cure between passes, and give the final layer a full dose
  2. Confirm the colour layer passes a thumbnail twist before varnishing
  3. Reduce dust (clean area, cover the wet part) and check ink limit for orange-peel
  4. Use a harder varnish or add a protective top coat for handled parts
  5. Check lamp with a radiometer; lower the head to the correct gap

Prevention

Treat varnish as a build material: cure each layer, test after cooling, and keep full-flood gloss away from dusty areas.

FIG. 07Missing lines, a white line through solids, or a fine line of misplaced dots; nozzle check shows gaps or a bent line.HighUV flatbed, roll-to-roll, rotary

Likely causes

  • Dried ink at the nozzle from poor capping or long stops
  • Air in the damper or a partly blocked ink filter
  • Physical damage to the nozzle plate (wiper, media strike, air gun)
  • Ink or cleaning fluid left in the head too long

First fixes

  1. Nozzle check, clean and purge; repeat up to three times, then stop and diagnose
  2. Check the cap seal and damper; replace the filter if flow does not recover
  3. Never use high-pressure air; replace the head if the nozzle plate is damaged
  4. Flush according to the maintenance schedule and cap the head whenever the machine stops

Prevention

Cap the head at every stop, keep cleaning liquid out of the head for more than 48 hours, and log recurring nozzle failures.

FIG. 08Small bumps, speckles or unprinted pin-holes in the print; dust sticks to the media and the head area.MediumUV flatbed, DTF

Likely causes

  • Dry air or low temperature increasing static build-up
  • Media not de-ionised before printing
  • Dusty workshop or media stored uncovered
  • Improper grounding of the machine

First fixes

  1. Wipe the media with IPA or use an ionising bar / static eliminator
  2. Raise humidity toward 35–65 % RH and keep the machine grounded
  3. Cover media between jobs and clean the head area at shift end
  4. Reject and reprint the affected sheet; do not try to fix pin-holes with more ink

Prevention

Keep a hygrometer in the shop, store media covered, and treat static as a process variable in dry months.

FIG. 09Media lifts or curls at the edges, the head scrapes the surface, and you see smears or a sudden height error.HighUV flatbed, roll-to-roll

Likely causes

  • Thin or heat-sensitive media deforming under lamp heat or vacuum
  • Vacuum too weak or the media not fully covered by the table zones
  • Head height set too low for the actual media thickness
  • Roll media not properly tensioned or de-curled

First fixes

  1. Stop the job, raise the head, and re-measure at the highest point
  2. Increase vacuum, tape down edges, or use a fixture for thin parts
  3. Pre-heat or flatten warped boards before printing
  4. Use a take-up/feed tension setting that keeps the media flat

Prevention

Check flatness and thickness before every new media batch, and treat a head strike as a head-damage event: inspect the nozzle plate immediately.

FIG. 10Prints start glossy but rub off, or look dull and feel tacky; cure settings that used to work now fail.HighUV flatbed, roll-to-roll, rotary

Likely causes

  • UV LED output decaying with age
  • Lamp window dirty with ink mist or dust
  • Dose too low for a thick white or varnish build
  • Print speed increased without adjusting lamp power

First fixes

  1. Measure with a radiometer and compare against the last service record
  2. Clean the lamp window and check the cooling airflow
  3. Slow down or raise lamp power within the manufacturer's limit
  4. Reduce the ink build or add a cure pass for thick layers

Prevention

Record lamp hours and radiometer readings at every service, and re-check cure after any change of media or ink.

FIG. 11Colour shifts, nozzle dropouts increase, ink smells different or prints with a stronger odour; filters clog faster.MediumUV flatbed, roll-to-roll, rotary

Likely causes

  • Ink past its shelf life, or stored outside the recommended temperature
  • Pigment settling and separation in old stock
  • Contamination from topping up an old cartridge with a different lot
  • Ink left in the machine for months without circulation

First fixes

  1. Record the lot on every cartridge, and use stock oldest-first
  2. Stop using suspect ink and flush the line before loading a new lot
  3. Replace filters and dampers if flow is already restricted
  4. Print a reference patch with the new lot before a paying job

Prevention

Store ink sealed and within the specified range, label open dates, and never mix lots without flushing.

FIG. 12The carriage moves to the wrong position, the job asks to 'continue printing', or the image shifts sideways between passes.HighUV flatbed

Likely causes

  • Dust, ink mist or a fingerprint on the encoder strip
  • Encoder strip scratched or not seated in its holder
  • Encoder sensor contaminated
  • Strip exposed to direct light or physical contact

First fixes

  1. Power off, then wipe the strip gently with a lint-free cloth and a little alcohol, in one direction only
  2. Check that the strip is clean, straight and properly seated
  3. Clean the sensor window; replace a scratched strip
  4. Re-run the position calibration and a test print before production

Prevention

Include the encoder strip in the weekly cleaning routine and keep the machine cover closed when it is not in use.
